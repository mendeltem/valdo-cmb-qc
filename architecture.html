Cerebral microbleed detection on T2*-GRE: from input to output

How one MRI volume becomes a list of microbleeds, which building blocks of the published reference pipeline (MicrobleedNet, Sundaresan et al. 2023) were kept, changed or removed, and what each decision was worth. All numbers: VALDO 2021 Task 2, 57 cases, 5-fold cross-validation, pooled lesion-level F1, out-of-fold. Status 21 September 2026. Tap a step to open it.

1  The pipeline, step by step

0Input: one T2*-weighted gradient-echo volumee.g. 512 x 512 x 35, 0.45 x 0.45 x 4 mm
VALDO Task 2 contains three cohorts with very different geometry: 0.45 x 0.45 x 4 mm (11 cases), 0.49 x 0.49 x 0.8 mm (34) and 1 x 1 x 3-4 mm (27); 72 cases, 236 annotated microbleeds. Microbleeds are small dark spots (2-10 mm). No other sequence is used: the target cohorts only have T2* or SWI.
↓
1Bias-field correction keptFSL FAST -B
Removes the slow brightness drift across the head. Not part of the original pipeline's code, but applied before it.
  • Ablation: training without it costs -0.022 F1.
↓
2Brain mask kept as deliveredimage > 0, holes filled
VALDO ships masked images, but only one cohort is really skull-stripped: measured mask volumes are 1419 / 1770 / 2533 ml, and 4 % / 16 % / 40 % of the mask lies outside the brain (eyes, skull base, scalp).
  • tested, no gain a tight mask from SynthSeg as network input: -0.048.
  • With a mask defined as image > 0, voxels of value 0 inside the brain (very dark lesions) fall outside the mask; holes are therefore filled.
↓
3Intensity scale: inverted, lesions bright kept1 - I / max(I)
As in the original. Two alternatives were tried:
  • tested, no gain z-score normalisation plus rotation / scaling / noise augmentation: -0.060.
  • open scaling by the 99.5th percentile instead of the maximum: +0.025 with an interval that includes zero; not pursued.
↓
4Vessel inpainting of the original pipeline removedFrangi + k-means per slice
The original paints over everything a vesselness filter finds, slice by slice, and rescues what looks round and solid. On thick slices a microbleed often does not -- a risk the authors name themselves; their pipeline was developed mainly on thin-slice SWI.
  • Audit against the labels: the inpainting touches 109 of 236 microbleeds, paints 27 over completely, leaves 17 invisible; it repaints 12-17 % of the brain.
  • Training without it: 0.470 → 0.588, paired +0.136 [+0.065; +0.226] -- the largest single gain of the project.
  • tested, no gain a repaired, label-free inpainting (scales in mm, one threshold per volume, only long thin structures) in three doses: 0.588 / 0.584 / 0.570.
↓
5Resampling to one training grid, cropped to the brain changed0.5 x 0.5 x 1.0 mm
The original works on the native grid with 48-voxel cubes, which are 22 mm wide but up to 190 mm tall on 4 mm slices. Here every case is brought to the same physical grid (image linear, label nearest neighbour; the centre of the field of view is preserved, which fixed a half-voxel shift of an earlier version).
  • Lesion volume survives (-1 / 0 / 0 %), lesion contrast does not (-26 / -15 / -30 % per cohort) -- interpolation blurs objects of a few voxels.
  • tested, no gain a coarser grid in z (2 mm): -0.036. The 1 mm grid stays.
↓
6Second input channel: radial symmetry (FRST) kept re-implemented2D per slice, radii 2 / 3 / 4 / 6 voxels = 1-3 mm
The fast radial symmetry transform (Loy & Zelinsky 2003) lights up small round blobs. The idea is the original's. The transform was re-implemented in floating point with a fixed scale across slices; in the public reference code (commit 958f1cb) gradients are cast to integers, directions are rounded by integer division and every slice is normalised by its own maximum, and two radii are used where the paper describes four.
  • The map peaks inside 96 % of the microbleeds (82 % with the original inpainting before it).
  • Ablation: T2* alone, without this channel, costs -0.053.
↓
7Patch sampling for training changed2 x 38 x 62 x 94 voxels = 38 x 31 x 47 mm
800 patches per epoch, batch 4: 60 % contain a microbleed at a random position (each lesion equally likely, so large ones do not dominate), 25 % random brain, 15 % anywhere; random flips along all three axes. The original uses non-overlapping 48-voxel cubes, balanced 1:1, and weights lesion voxels ten-fold in the loss.
  • unstable only 33 % lesion patches (1:2): training collapses to "predict nothing" -- the smoothed Dice term rewards an empty output on empty patches. The share of lesion patches is what keeps this recipe stable.
  • open smaller (24 mm) and larger (64 mm) patches are queued as a context series.
↓
8Network: anisotropic 3D U-Net changed22.5 M parameters
Winner of a tournament of nine single-stage networks (attention U-Net, nnU-Net-style, 2.5D, residual-SE, U-Net++, shallow-dilated, Swin UNETR, SegResNet) under one recipe: 0.245 → 0.404 against the 3D attention U-Net, paired +0.159 [+0.091; +0.234], ahead in all three cohorts. The one idea: at full resolution the grid is 0.5 x 0.5 x 1 mm and much of the through-plane direction is interpolated, so the first level convolves and pools only in-plane; after that the grid is 1 mm isotropic and the network is an ordinary 3D U-Net. Details in section 2.
  • tested, no gain attention gates on the winner (40 epochs).
  • tested, no gain tissue map (SynthSeg) as five extra input channels: the network learns the CSF rule by itself (no prediction in CSF any more) but makes more false alarms elsewhere, -0.058.
↓
9Loss, optimiser, schedule changedBCE + Dice, AdamW 3e-4, cosine, 60 epochs
InstanceNorm, mixed precision. The schedule always runs 60 epochs (early stopping made results depend on the random seed by about 0.08 F1); the checkpoint used for prediction is the one with the best F1 on the inner fold, checked every 5 epochs. A run that uses the last checkpoint instead is queued.
  • tested, no gain instance-wise "blob" loss: sensitivity for small lesions 0.52 → 0.64, but more false alarms, F1 -0.044 (second seed -0.063).
  • tested, no gain auxiliary centre-map head (after CenSynCMB 2026): small lesions 0.52 → 0.66, F1 -0.054.
  • tested, no gain re-weighting towards missed lesions: -0.030; online hard-negative mining: ±0.000 (false alarms are unlearned on the training cases only).
  • Common pattern: everything that makes the network more sensitive is paid for with false alarms. The bottleneck is telling microbleeds from mimics with a single sequence.
↓
10Inference: sliding window over the whole volumeprobability map, same grid
Windows of the training size with half-window stride, overlapping predictions averaged, windows outside the brain skipped; sigmoid gives a probability per voxel.
  • Mean of two random seeds: as good as the better member and +0.02 to +0.04 above an average member; no further gain from more members.
  • Mirror test-time augmentation (8 views): +0.02 on VALDO.
↓
11Post-processing: one fixed operating point changedp > 0.3 → components → size → CSF rule
  • threshold 0.3, 26-connected components;
  • drop components smaller than 2 mm³ (8 voxels) or larger than 2100 voxels (a 10 mm sphere);
  • CSF rule: drop a component whose majority SynthSeg label is ventricle or CSF -- parameter-free, 0.585 → 0.646 at this operating point.
The original filters by volume, ellipticity and distance to the mask border with thresholds set per dataset. Here one setting is fixed for all runs; re-tuning it per model on 57 cases moves the score by 0.02-0.05 and is not used for decisions.
↓
12Outputmask + list of lesions
A binary microbleed mask on the training grid, the number of lesions, and for each lesion its size and location (lobar / deep / infratentorial, from the SynthSeg map); optionally a combined map of grey matter, white matter, CSF and microbleeds. A quality-control page shows reference and prediction side by side for every case.
↓
13Optional second stage: look at every candidate again not confirmed3 x 16 x 32 x 32 → yes / no
Candidates are the components above a low threshold (0.15). A small plain 3D CNN (three double-convolution blocks with 16 / 32 / 64 channels, global average, 0.22 M parameters; input T2*, FRST and the first stage's probability in a 16 mm cube centred on the candidate) or a random forest on ten physical features plus the tissue class decides per candidate. One classifier is trained for all cohorts, evaluated with nested cross-validation.
  • On VALDO it equals the simple rule above (0.62-0.63); it learns the CSF rule without being given it.
  • A gain on another cohort did not reproduce with an independently trained first stage, so the second stage is not part of the pipeline yet.

Honest reading: 0.646 is the better of two random seeds (the other reaches 0.590); six nearly identical runs average 0.61, and the mean of two seeds reliably gives about 0.64. With the challenge's own rule (median F1 per subject, centroids within 5 mm) the model reaches 0.667 in cross-validation; the best challenge teams reported 0.667-0.684 on a hidden test set, and CenSynCMB (2026, three sequences) 0.743 per-subject mean in cross-validation, where this model reaches 0.57-0.63. The gap is precision. Fifteen VALDO cases are held out for one final measurement; they were looked at once, early in the project, which will be disclosed.

5  How it is evaluated

  • 72 cases: 15 held out (97 lesions), 57 in five stratified folds (139 lesions). For fold k, another fold selects checkpoint and threshold; fold k is only predicted.
  • Lesion-level F1 pooled over cases: 26-connected components, a reference lesion counts as found when a predicted component touches it; every predicted component that touches none is a false alarm.
  • Every comparison is paired over the same cases (bootstrap over cases, 10 000 draws, 95 % interval). "No gain" means the interval includes zero or lies below it.
  • Transfer to two private cohorts (T2* with 5 mm slices, SWI): each sequence needs its own model; training on the target cohort and fine-tuning the VALDO model are equivalent on T2*; zero-shot use and joint training of all cohorts are measurably worse. The network finds the same lesions across cohorts -- what does not transfer is what is not a lesion.

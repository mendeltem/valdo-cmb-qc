Cerebral microbleed detection on T2*-GRE: from input to output

How one MRI volume becomes a list of microbleeds, which building blocks of the published reference pipeline (MicrobleedNet, Sundaresan et al. 20231) were kept, changed or removed, and what each decision was worth. All numbers: VALDO 2021 Task 22, 57 cases, 5-fold cross-validation, pooled lesion-level F1, out-of-fold, paired against the previous version of the chain with a 95 % bootstrap interval in brackets. Superscript numbers point to the reference list at the end; each entry there says what is taken from the source. Status 7 October 2026. Tap a step to open it.

kept as in the original (or standard practice); the ablation number is given where one exists changed differs from the original: the step states what the original does, what is done here instead, why, and what the paired measurement showed removed taken out because it measurably hurt tested, no gain tried; the interval included zero or lay below it open not measured, or measured only within the noise of a single random seed (about 0.08 F1)

Reading rule for every number on this page: a difference is confirmed only if its 95 % interval excludes zero, or if it reproduced with a second random seed. Everything else is a direction, not a result. Where a step says "within noise", it stays in the chain only because removing it was not better either.

1  The pipeline, step by step

0Input: one T2*-weighted gradient-echo volume, as deliverede.g. 512 x 512 x 35, 0.45 x 0.45 x 4 mm
VALDO Task 22 contains three cohorts with very different geometry: 0.45 x 0.45 x 4 mm (11 cases), 0.49 x 0.49 x 0.8 mm (34) and 1 x 1 x 3-4 mm (27); 72 cases, 236 annotated microbleeds. Microbleeds are small round hypointense foci of 2-10 mm3. No other sequence is used: the target cohorts only have T2* or SWI.
  • Brain mask: the one that comes with the data. VALDO ships non-brain voxels set to zero. Only one cohort is really skull-stripped: 4 % / 16 % / 40 % of the delivered mask lies outside the brain (eyes, skull base, scalp). The mask used here is image > 0 with holes filled, because a very dark voxel inside the brain can be exactly zero and would otherwise drop out. No microbleed is cut off by the delivered mask.
  • tested, no gain a tight brain mask from SynthSeg as network input: -0.048 [-0.099; +0.009]. Masking is therefore not a step of this pipeline.
↓
1Intensity: bias-field correction, then the original's inverted scale keptFSL FAST -B → 1 - I / max(I)
Original
Inverts the image inside the mask, 1 - I / max(I), so that microbleeds become the brightest objects1. Nothing else.
Here
The same inversion, applied to the output of FSL FAST with the -B option4, which estimates a smooth multiplicative brightness field across the head and divides it out.
Why
Scaling by the maximum is sensitive to where the brightest voxel sits; a slow brightness drift moves it. The correction was in place before the first measurement and the chain was built on it.
Measured
Training without the bias-field correction: -0.022 [-0.097; +0.052]. open within noise -- it is not shown to matter, and a future version may drop it.
  • tested, no gain z-score normalisation plus rotation / scaling / noise augmentation: -0.060; scaling by the 99.5th percentile instead of the maximum: +0.025 with an interval that includes zero; histogram landmark matching across cohorts: 0.549 against 0.646. All affine intensity scales are equivalent here; the intensity path is closed.
↓
2Vessel inpainting of the original pipeline removedFrangi + k-means per slice
Original
Before the network sees the image, everything a vesselness filter5 finds is painted over with surrounding intensities, slice by slice; blobs that look round and solid are rescued1. The authors name the risk themselves: on thick slices a microbleed often does not look round and solid in 2D. Their pipeline was developed mainly on thin-slice SWI.
Here
No inpainting. The network sees the bias-corrected, inverted image.
Why
Audit against the labels: the inpainting touches 109 of 236 microbleeds, paints 27 over completely, leaves 17 invisible, and repaints 12-17 % of the brain. A step that deletes the target cannot be repaired by the network.
Measured
Training without it: 0.470 → 0.588, paired +0.136 [+0.065; +0.226] -- the largest single gain of the project.
  • tested, no gain a repaired, label-free inpainting (scales in mm, one threshold per volume, only long thin structures) in three doses: 0.588 / 0.584 / 0.570.
↓
3One physical grid for every case, cropped to the brain changed0.5 x 0.5 x 1.0 mm
Original
Works on the native voxel grid with 48-voxel cubes1; on the 4 mm cohort such a cube is 22 mm wide but 190 mm tall.
Here
Every case is resampled to 0.5 x 0.5 x 1.0 mm (image linear, label nearest neighbour; the centre of the field of view is preserved, which fixed a half-voxel shift of an earlier version) and cropped to the bounding box of the brain.
Why
A convolution kernel should mean the same millimetres in every case; with slices from 0.8 to 4 mm it does not on the native grid. This is the same argument nnU-Net makes for a common target spacing6. The price is contrast: lesion volume survives the interpolation (-1 / 0 / 0 % per cohort), lesion peak contrast does not (-26 / -15 / -30 %), because the objects are only a few voxels wide.
Measured
A coarser grid in z (2 mm): -0.036 [-0.105; +0.032]; cubic instead of linear interpolation: -0.024 (two seeds). Neither direction is confirmed; the 1 mm grid stays as the middle.
↓
4Second input channel: fast radial symmetry (FRST) kept, re-implemented2D per slice, radii 2 / 3 / 4 / 6 voxels = 1-3 mm
Source
Loy & Zelinsky7: every pixel votes along its gradient direction at a distance r; votes accumulate at the centres of small round blobs, so the map peaks on microbleeds and stays flat on edges and long vessels. The original uses this map as a second channel next to the image1.
Here
Re-implemented in floating point with four radii in millimetres and one fixed scale for the whole volume. In the public reference code (commit 958f1cb) gradients are cast to integers, directions are rounded by integer division, every slice is normalised by its own maximum (noise becomes signal in slices without a lesion), and two radii are used where the paper describes four.
Measured
The map peaks inside 96 % of the microbleeds (82 % when the original inpainting runs first). Training on T2* alone, without the channel: -0.053 [-0.116; +0.004], lower in all five folds -- a consistent direction whose interval just touches zero.
↓
5Network: anisotropic 3D U-Net changed22.5 M parameters
Original
A 3D U-Net-like encoder-decoder on 48-voxel cubes as candidate detector1; the first re-implementation here used a 3D attention U-Net with the original recipe and reached F1 0.245.
Here
A 3D U-Net8,9 whose first level convolves (1 x 3 x 3) and pools (1 x 2 x 2) in-plane only; from the next level on the grid is 1 mm isotropic and the network is an ordinary 3D U-Net (32 base channels, four poolings, InstanceNorm10, LeakyReLU, transposed convolutions, one sigmoid output). Diagram in section 2.
Why
At 0.5 x 0.5 x 1 mm most through-plane samples are interpolated copies of their neighbours; averaging them in the first layer adds no information and halves the useful in-plane resolution. nnU-Net applies the same rule to anisotropic data6.
Measured
Winner of a tournament of nine single-stage networks under one recipe (attention U-Net, nnU-Net-style, 2.5D, residual-SE, U-Net++, shallow-dilated, Swin UNETR, SegResNet): 0.245 → 0.404 against the attention U-Net, paired +0.159 [+0.091; +0.234], ahead in all three cohorts. A second tournament at 60 epochs (2 October 2026) confirmed it: MedNeXt -0.098 [-0.175; -0.018], nnU-Net residual encoder -0.063 [-0.143; -0.002], R2U-Net 2.5D -0.141 [-0.213; -0.067], width 16 / 48 channels -0.051 / -0.112.
  • tested, no gain attention gates on the winner: -0.056 [-0.120; +0.004] -- the gates are not what hurt the first network, the isotropic first level was.
  • tested, no gain a SynthSeg tissue map as five extra input channels: the network learns the CSF rule of step 8 by itself but makes more false alarms elsewhere, -0.058.
↓
6Training recipe: patches, loss, optimiser, schedule changed800 patches / epoch, 60 epochs
Original
Non-overlapping 48-voxel cubes, lesion and non-lesion cubes balanced 1:1, lesion voxels weighted ten-fold in the loss, up to 100 epochs with early stopping on a validation split1.
Here
800 random patches of 2 x 38 x 62 x 94 voxels (38 x 31 x 47 mm) per epoch, batch 4: 60 % centred on a microbleed at a random offset (each lesion equally likely, so large ones do not dominate), 25 % random brain, 15 % anywhere; random flips along all axes. Loss 0.5 binary cross-entropy + 0.5 soft Dice11, no voxel weights. AdamW, learning rate 3e-4, weight decay 1e-412; cosine decay to zero over a fixed 60 epochs13; bfloat16 mixed precision14. The checkpoint used for prediction is the one with the best lesion F1 on the inner fold, checked every 5 epochs.
Why
Two things keep this recipe stable. The lesion share: with 33 % lesion patches (the nnU-Net default is a third6) training collapsed to "predict nothing", because the smoothed Dice term rewards an empty output on empty patches. The fixed length: with early stopping the result depended on the random seed by about 0.08 F1; the fixed schedule took 0.404 → 0.470.
Measured
Everything below was tried against this recipe and did not raise F1:
  • tested, no gain blob loss15 (a Dice term computed per connected lesion instead of over the whole patch, so small lesions count as much as large ones; here with the paper's weighting alpha = 2, beta = 1, 26-connected components): sensitivity for small lesions 0.52 → 0.64, but more false alarms, F1 -0.044 (second seed -0.063).
  • tested, no gain auxiliary centre-map head and false-negative re-weighting after CenSynCMB16: small lesions 0.52 → 0.66, F1 -0.054 [-0.110; -0.006]; re-weighting alone -0.030.
  • tested, no gain online hard-negative mining: ±0.000; 90 or 120 epochs: -0.016 / -0.022; patches of 24 mm or 48 mm: -0.216 / -0.037; focal Tversky loss: -0.355; learning rate 1e-3 or 1e-4: -0.180 / -0.086; batch 2 or 8, weight decay 0 or 1e-3, 400 or 1600 patches: all within ±0.09 with intervals over zero; last checkpoint instead of the best inner-fold checkpoint: -0.023 [-0.069; +0.012].
  • Common pattern: everything that makes the network more sensitive is paid for with false alarms. The bottleneck is telling microbleeds from mimics (vessel cross-sections, calcification) with a single magnitude sequence.
↓
7Inference: sliding window, mean of two seeds keptprobability map, same grid
Windows of the training size slide over the whole volume with half-window stride; overlapping predictions are averaged, windows outside the brain are skipped; the sigmoid gives a probability per voxel6. The final map is the mean of two networks trained with different random seeds.
  • Measured: the mean of two seeds is as good as the better member and +0.02 to +0.04 above an average member (0.647 against 0.646 / 0.590); three members 0.648, five 0.630 -- no further gain from more members.
  • tested, no gain mirror test-time augmentation (8 views): +0.019 [-0.017; +0.058]; not used.
↓
8Post-processing: one fixed operating point and an anatomical rule changedp > 0.3 → components → size → CSF rule
Original
Candidates are filtered by volume, ellipticity and distance to the brain-mask border, with thresholds chosen per dataset1.
Here
One setting for every run: threshold 0.3; 26-connected components; drop components smaller than 2 mm³ (8 voxels) or larger than 2100 voxels (a 10 mm sphere); CSF rule: drop a component whose majority tissue label in a SynthSeg segmentation17 of the same case is ventricle or extracerebral CSF.
Why
98 of 176 false alarms lay in free cerebrospinal fluid, where a microbleed cannot be (it is a parenchymal lesion3). The rule has no tunable parameter and removes no true lesion. A fixed operating point, rather than one re-tuned per model, keeps the comparisons on this page honest: re-tuning threshold and size per model on 57 cases moves the score by 0.02-0.05.
Measured
0.585 → 0.646 at the fixed operating point, 0 of 139 lesions lost. Cross-selected (threshold chosen on the other folds) the chain scores 0.622.
↓
9Outputmask + list of lesions
A binary microbleed mask on the training grid, the number of lesions, and for each lesion its size and location (lobar / deep / infratentorial, from the SynthSeg map); optionally a combined map of grey matter, white matter, CSF and microbleeds. The quality-control pages of this site show reference and prediction side by side for every case.
↓
10Optional second stage: look at every candidate again confirmed on SWI only3 x 16 x 32 x 32 → yes / no
Original
A second network (24-voxel cubes) classifies every candidate; a multi-task teacher guides it by knowledge distillation1,18. Trained per dataset.
Here
Candidates are the components above a low threshold (0.15), without the CSF rule. A small plain 3D CNN (three double-convolution blocks with 16 / 32 / 64 channels, global average pooling, 0.22 M parameters) sees T2*, FRST and the first stage's probability in a 16 mm cube around the candidate, plus the tissue class, and answers yes / no. One classifier is trained on the candidates of all cohorts together and evaluated with nested cross-validation.
Why
Per cohort the candidate sets were too small to learn from; pooled they are not. The classifier learns the CSF rule without being told and can weigh soft tissue evidence where a hard rule deletes true lesions (on SWI the rule removes 74 real microbleeds).
Measured
Against threshold + CSF rule: SWI +0.044 [+0.012; +0.085] (reproduced with a second seed and three classifier families); T2* +0.001; VALDO -0.003 -- equal to the rule. A classifier trained on VALDO candidates only transfers without adaptation (SWI 0.671 against 0.672 for the pooled one); fine-tuning it with 10-25 % of the target cases hurts. External check on a public SWI cohort19 (57 cases, 146 microbleeds, nothing trained on it): first stage + CSF rule 0.613, with the VALDO-trained classifier 0.630, median F1 per subject 0.667.

Honest reading: 0.646 is the better of two random seeds (the other reaches 0.590); six nearly identical runs average 0.61, and the mean of two seeds reliably gives about 0.64 (0.647). With the challenge's own rule (median F1 per subject, centroids within 5 mm) the model reaches 0.667 in cross-validation; the best challenge teams reported 0.667-0.684 on a hidden test set2, and CenSynCMB (three sequences) 0.743 per-subject mean in cross-validation16, where this model reaches 0.57-0.63. The gap is precision.
Fifteen VALDO cases were held out from all development and measured once, on 28 September 2026, with the frozen chain: pooled F1 0.468. One case carries 73 of the 97 lesions (small, low-contrast; sensitivity 0.20 there); without it 0.731, median F1 per case 0.667. The chain is not over-fitted to its folds; its limit is a heavy load of small microbleeds. These cases were looked at once early in the project, before the chain was built.

5  How it is evaluated

  • 72 cases: 15 held out (97 lesions), 57 in five stratified folds (139 lesions). For fold k, another fold selects checkpoint and threshold; fold k is only predicted.
  • Lesion-level F1 pooled over cases: 26-connected components, a reference lesion counts as found when a predicted component touches it; every predicted component that touches none is a false alarm. Detection of small sparse objects is an instance problem, so the metric counts lesions, not voxels20.
  • Every comparison is paired over the same cases (bootstrap over cases21, 10 000 draws, 95 % interval). "No gain" means the interval includes zero or lies below it. A single U-Net training scatters by about 0.08 F1 between random seeds; decisions below that size need a second seed.
  • Transfer to two private cohorts (T2* with 5 mm slices, 75 cases; SWI, 61 cases): each sequence needs its own first-stage model; training on the target cohort and fine-tuning the VALDO model are equivalent on T2*; zero-shot use and joint training of all cohorts are measurably worse. The network finds the same lesions across cohorts -- what does not transfer is what is not a lesion. The classifier of step 10 does transfer.
  • External validation on a public SWI cohort of 57 cases19, nothing trained on it: F1 0.613-0.630 pooled, median 0.667 per subject (section 1, step 10).

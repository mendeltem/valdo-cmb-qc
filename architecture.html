Cerebral microbleed detection on T2*-GRE and SWI: how the pipeline was built

This page tells the story of the pipeline in the order it happened. We started with a clinical question and no method, read what the literature offers, put the candidate methods into one arena on the public VALDO data and let a single number, the lesion-level F1, decide. The anisotropic 3D U-Net won. With the network fixed, every pre-processing and post-processing idea from the literature was measured on top of it, one at a time, and kept only if it scored higher. The chain that came out of VALDO was then carried over to the in-house Charité cohorts (T2* and SWI). Superscript numbers point to the reference list; each entry there says what was taken from the source. Every measured number links to the section of the public lab book that records it (lab book, table of all tests, commands). Statements for which no source exists have been removed. Reading rule for every number on this page: all scores are pooled lesion-level F1 on the 57 VALDO cross-validation cases (5 folds, out-of-fold), paired against the stated comparison with a 95 % bootstrap interval over cases in brackets. A difference is confirmed only if that interval excludes zero or if it reproduced with a second random seed. Everything else is a direction, not a result.

1  The question and the data

Cerebral microbleeds are small round or ovoid hypointense foci of 2-10 mm in brain parenchyma on T2*-weighted gradient-echo or susceptibility-weighted MRI; their mimics are vessel cross-sections, calcification, iron deposits, bone and air3. The clinical cohorts this pipeline is for have one such sequence per patient and nothing else: 75 T2* cases with 828 reference microbleeds and 61 SWI cases with 652, read at the Charité. The method therefore had to work from a single magnitude image. The current best published result on VALDO uses three sequences16, which rules it out here.

Development data: VALDO 2021 Task 22, 72 T2*-GRE cases from three cohorts with 236 annotated microbleeds and very different geometry (0.45 x 0.45 x 4 mm, 11 cases; 0.49 x 0.49 x 0.8 mm, 34; 1 x 1 x 3-4 mm, 27). 15 cases were set aside and measured once at the end; the other 57 (139 lesions) carry every decision in five stratified folds, where for fold k another fold selects checkpoint and threshold and fold k is only predicted commands 4.

The yardstick: detection of small, sparse, countable objects is an instance problem, so the metric counts lesions, not voxels20. A reference lesion counts as found when a predicted 26-connected component touches it; every component that touches none is a false alarm; F1 is pooled over cases. Every comparison is paired over the same cases with a bootstrap21 (10 000 draws). One U-Net training scatters by about 0.08 F1 between random seeds (three seeds of the identical recipe: 0.646 / 0.590 / 0.578 lab book 5u), so decisions below that size need a second seed; with about 60 cases, error bars of this size are expected46.

↗Why 60 fixed epochs and not early stopping: loss and inner-fold F1 at 60 / 90 / 120 epochstap to open

Round 1 trained with early stopping on the inner-fold F1 (checked every 5 epochs, patience 3 checks, at most 40 epochs). That F1 scatters by about 0.08 between random seeds, so the stop fires on noise: with the second seed one fold kept the checkpoint of epoch 5 and stopped at epoch 20, and the run scored 0.321 instead of 0.404 (lab book 4.5). A fixed cosine schedule of 60 epochs took the recipe to 0.470 and removed the seed dependence of the length. Longer is not better: at 90 and 120 epochs the training loss keeps falling and the best inner-fold value creeps up (it is a maximum over more checks), but the out-of-fold score does not follow; the paired result is -0.016 [-0.091; +0.060] for 90 and -0.029 on VALDO / -0.022 confirmed on the in-house T2* cohort for 120 (lab book 5h, round 2). The checkpoint is still chosen on the inner fold; taking the last state instead costs -0.023 [-0.069; +0.012], within noise (lab book 5o), which is what the full-data models of the product do.

Used

8Post-processing: one fixed operating point and an anatomical rule chosen by paired measurementp > 0.3 → components → size → CSF rule
Methods available
Threshold and connected components; filters by size, shape and distance to the mask border with thresholds tuned per dataset1; anatomy: a microbleed is parenchymal3, so a component in free cerebrospinal fluid can be dropped, with the CSF taken from SynthSeg17,37 (a CSF / white-matter mask as location feature is also used by Myung et al.38); or hand the candidates to a learned second stage (step 9).
How chosen
Threshold and size were chosen once on the inner folds and then frozen for every model; re-tuning them per model on 57 cases moves the score by 0.02-0.05 and would make the comparisons on this page dishonest. The CSF rule was audited against the reference labels before it was switched on.
Used
Threshold 0.3; 26-connected components; drop components smaller than 2 mm³ or larger than 2100 voxels (a 10 mm sphere); drop a component whose majority tissue label is ventricle or extracerebral CSF. No tunable parameter.
Measured
98 of 176 false alarms lay in free cerebrospinal fluid. 0.585 → 0.646 at the fixed operating point, 0 of 139 lesions lost; cross-selected 0.622 lab book 4.3. Further hand-made location and shape rules against vessels brought nothing lab book 5i.

Honest reading: 0.646 is the better of two random seeds (the other reaches 0.590); the mean of two seeds gives 0.647 lab book 5u. With the challenge's own rule (median F1 per subject, centroids within 5 mm) the model reaches 0.667 in cross-validation; the best challenge teams reported 0.667-0.684 on a hidden test set2, and CenSynCMB (three sequences) 0.74316.
The 15 VALDO cases held out from all development were measured once, on 28 September 2026, with the frozen chain: pooled F1 0.468. One case carries 73 of the 97 lesions (small, low-contrast; sensitivity 0.20 there); without it 0.731, median F1 per case 0.667 lab book 5ad. The chain is not over-fitted to its folds; its limit is a heavy load of small microbleeds.

6  From VALDO to the Charité cohorts

The chain was built on VALDO and then carried over to the two in-house cohorts. The question was how: use the VALDO network as it is, mix the cohorts, fine-tune, or train the same recipe per cohort lab book 5e, 5h. Only pooled scores of the in-house cohorts appear on this page.

The network finds the same lesions across cohorts; what does not transfer is what is not a lesion. Each sequence therefore gets its own first-stage network, while the second-stage classifier transfers, even when trained on VALDO candidates only (SWI 0.671 against 0.672 for the pooled one). The chain can thus be trained from public data alone.

External check on a public SWI cohort19 (57 cases, 146 microbleeds, nothing trained on it): VALDO network alone 0.525, with the CSF rule 0.613, SWI network with the VALDO-trained classifier 0.630 (+0.106 confirmed against the rule), median F1 per subject 0.667 lab book 5ae, arm 4.
